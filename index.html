Kaarigar notes

A saree that took nineteen days should not be sold in ninety seconds

RoopKala began in 2011 as two tables and a hand-pump on Shastri Maidan Road. We still buy the way we started: walk to Bishanpur for the Sujani quilting, take the morning bus to Bhagalpur for tussar, and sit with the weaver until the lot is chosen. Nothing reaches the floor without a name attached to it.

What you see here is our display catalogue — real prices, real stock, photographed on the same day it hung on the rack. To feel the weave, the counter is open till 8:30.

The running stitch

Sujani is quilted, not printed — and you can feel every stitch

A Sujani panel begins as three layers of old cloth basted together. The women of Bishanpur work a running stitch through all three, and the pattern rises out of the gathering: fish for the river, lotus for the pond, bamboo for the wedding, and a border of tiny chain that takes an evening per hand-span.

We buy these panels the way they are made — one at a time, from the household that stitched them. Nothing here is machine reproduction, and we will tell you at the counter which pieces are Sujani and which are merely inspired by it.
